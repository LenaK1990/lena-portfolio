Product context

Kreddy is a B2C personal finance app that helps users understand their credit score, existing loans, and overall financial health. Its loan application allows users to consolidate existing loans when applying for a new one.

The app uses credit and consumer data from UC, a Swedish data provider. We collaborated with UC to define the data requirements for this experience.

Business goal

Increase the number of submitted loan applications where users choose to consolidate their existing loans.

Challenge

Users could already view their credit score and existing loans in the app, but this information wasn't available when they made the consolidation decision.

The challenge was to bring relevant financial information into the loan application without disrupting the existing flow or making consolidation feel mandatory.

Previous experience

Constraints

  • Show relevant UC financial data, including mortgages, while keeping mortgage information separate from loans that can be consolidated.
  • Keep consolidation optional and neutral, without encouraging users to consolidate.
  • Preserve the existing application flow without fundamentally changing its structure.

Early exploration

I explored bringing UC credit data into the first application step. However, making the information conditional on the consolidation toggle introduced additional interaction logic into the existing form.

Two directions

Based on feedback, we moved away from changing the first-step logic and focused on solutions within its existing structure.

Sticky UC data banner

  • ✓︎

    Stays outside the main form flow and remains accessible throughout the journey

  • ⚠︎

    May compete with the primary CTA and take up valuable screen real estate

Compact UC data card

  • ✓︎

    Places UC data directly before the consolidation decision while keeping it distinct from the consolidation control

  • ⚠︎

    Only visible at the point of decision

I proposed Direction 2 – the UC data compact card – to make UC data relevant at the point of decision without adding persistent UI. Together with the product team, we defined the final solution and took it into the A/B test.

Success metric

Experiment note

Primary analysis: 86% statistical significance — below the CRO team’s 90% threshold.

Supplementary analysis: 96% statistical significance after swapping the baseline and variation, supporting the rollout recommendation.

Reflection

The A/B test showed an impact, but not what drives the consolidation decision. A natural next step would be qualitative research to uncover users’ concerns, information needs, and decision factors.
